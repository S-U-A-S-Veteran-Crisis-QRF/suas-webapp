Why food, ride & shelter

HALT — stopping crisis at the trigger point

In peer support and recovery, four states are known to push a person toward the edge: being Hungry, Angry, Lonely, or Tired. Together they spell HALT — and the word is the instruction: when you notice them, halt. For an isolated veteran these are the quiet conditions that turn a hard night into an emergency. The app is designed to interrupt them — so a tap can meet the basic human need behind a trigger before it can escalate.

H — Hungry

Designed so a veteran can get a hot meal, delivered free — no empty stomach left to sharpen a bad night.

A — Angry

Designed to connect a veteran with a peer who's been there, and help that comes fast, so frustration doesn't compound in isolation.

L — Lonely

Designed so a veteran can get a ride to connection and reach a fellow veteran on the other end. Isolation is the risk; presence is the answer.

T — Tired

Designed so a veteran can find a safe place to sleep tonight, no payment. Rest, before exhaustion becomes despair.

The design intent: meet the need and the trigger loses its power. This isn't therapy or a hotline — it's the practical step designed to keep HALT from becoming the last straw. For immediate danger, the Veterans Crisis Line (988, Press 1) is always one tap away.
